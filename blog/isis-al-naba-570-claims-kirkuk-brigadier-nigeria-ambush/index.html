ISIS’s al-Naba Claims an Iraqi General and a Nigerian Camp Commander in Issue 570

Oct 23, 2026
InvestigationOSINTCounterterrorismIraqNigeria

By Colin Aamot

Share

What did ISIS claim in al-Naba issue 570, and do officials confirm it?

Al-Naba issue 570, the Islamic State’s weekly newspaper dated October 22, 2026, claims an October 14 bomb in Kirkuk’s Wadi Zghaytoun that killed an Iraqi brigadier general and an October 19 ambush in Nigeria’s Yobe State that killed a camp commander. Iraqi and Nigerian accounts confirm both deaths but differ with ISIS on key details.

The Islamic State’s weekly newspaper came out on schedule again on Thursday, October 22, 2026. Issue 570 of al-Naba leads with a claim of “dozens” of Nigerian and Nigerien troops killed in West Africa. Its most specific claim, though, is a bomb in northern Iraq. The group says it killed a brigadier general of the Iraqi army and his aide in Kirkuk province on October 14.

Iraq’s Defense Ministry has confirmed the officer’s death, and his name. It has not confirmed the ISIS version of how he died.

The issue also carries an Amaq News Agency claim, repeated in its West Africa pages, that ISIS fighters killed a Nigerian army camp commander and six soldiers in a roadside ambush in Yobe State on October 19. Nigerian newspapers and AFP reported a deadly ambush near Geidam that day.

Editor’s note: The Islamic State is a designated Foreign Terrorist Organization. This report does not name or link any ISIS-run domain, channel, bot, wallet or file; sites are identified by outlet or role only. All casualty figures attributed to ISIS, al-Naba or Amaq are the group’s claims.

Key Takeaways

  • Al-Naba issue 570 claims ISIS fighters detonated a bomb on an Iraqi army patrol in Wadi Zghaytoun, in Kirkuk’s al-Rashad area, on October 14, 2026, killing a brigadier general it names as “Ahat Yasir Husayn” and his aide.
  • Iraq’s Defense Ministry named the dead as Brig. Gen. Ahat Yasser Khudair Hussein and Warrant Officer Mutaz Shahid Abbas, but blamed an explosive device left behind by ISIS, found while troops inspected the site of an Iraqi airstrike.
  • In Nigeria, ISIS claims a Yobe ambush killed the commander of the Kanamma camp and “at least six” soldiers. Nigerian press reported six soldiers, including a major who was the commanding officer, were killed; AFP’s military source said at least five.
  • The issue’s weekly tally claims 18 operations and more than 57 killed and wounded from October 15 to 21. West Africa accounts for 11 operations and 46 of the claimed casualties. Syria does not appear at all.
  • The issue’s editorial argues that its enemies’ campaigns have “failed,” three weeks after the U.S. military mission in Iraq ended.

The Kirkuk Claim: A General in Wadi Zghaytoun

Al-Naba runs the Iraq item on its front page and in full on page five, under a headline that puns on the officer’s first name. “Ahat” is Arabic for “groans,” and the headline reads, “The Rafidites’ groans continue.” “Rafidite” is the group’s slur for Shiites. The full item reads:

آهات الرافضة تتواصل.. مقتل (عميد) في الجيش الرافضي ومساعده بتفجير جديد في كركوك

أطاح مجاهدو الدولة الإسلامية في ولاية العراق أواخر الأسبوع الماضي بضابط بارز في الجيش الرافضي ومساعده وأصابوا آخرين بجروح، بتفجير جديد استهدف حملة عسكرية للعدو جنوب غرب كركوك.

وفي التفاصيل، قال مصدر خاص لـ(النبأ) إن جنود الدولة الإسلامية فجّروا عبوة ناسفة في صباح يوم الأربعاء (٣/جمادى الأولى) على دورية للجيش الرافضي المرتد كانت تقوم بعمليات تمشيط لإحدى القرى في وادي (زغيتون) بمنطقة (الرشاد).

وأضاف المصدر أن التفجير أسفر عن مقتل ضابط برتبة (عميد) يدعى "آهات ياسر حسين" برفقة مساعد له وإصابة عنصرين آخرين بجروح إضافة إلى تدمير آليتهم التي ضربها التفجير بشكل مباشر، ولله الحمد.

واعترف الجيش الرافضي في بيان له بمقتل الضابط ومساعده وذكر بيان العدو أن الضابط يشغل منصب "آمر آمرية اتصالات فرقة المشاة الحادية عشر" على حد وصف البيان.

وكانت الدورية تحاول الوصول إلى موقع للمجاهدين تعرض لقصف جوي سابق في الوادي الشهير الذي كان شاهدا على العديد من الإخفاقات الأمريكية والرافضية المشتركة طوال السنوات الماضية.

وتحرص القوات الرافضية عادة على الوصول إلى مواقع القصف هذه بحثا عن أي صورة للإنجاز تحت أجنحة الطائرات الأمريكية.

يذكر أن المجاهدين في ولاية العراق قد شنوا عدة هجمات خلال الأسابيع الأخيرة في مناطق مختلفة بما فيها كركوك، أسقطت عددا من القتلى والجرحى في صفوف القوات الرافضية بينهم ضباط.

The Rafidites’ groans continue.. Killing of a (brigadier) in the Rafidite army and his aide in a new bombing in Kirkuk

The mujahideen of the Islamic State in Iraq Province took down, late last week, a prominent officer in the Rafidite army and his aide, and wounded others, in a new bombing that targeted a military campaign of the enemy southwest of Kirkuk.

In detail, a special source told (al-Naba) that soldiers of the Islamic State detonated an explosive device on Wednesday morning (3 Jumada al-Ula) on a patrol of the apostate Rafidite army that was carrying out sweep operations of one of the villages in Wadi (Zghaytoun) in the (al-Rashad) area.

The source added that the explosion resulted in the killing of an officer with the rank of (brigadier) named “Ahat Yasir Husayn,” along with an aide of his, and the wounding of two other members, in addition to the destruction of their vehicle, which the explosion struck directly, praise be to God.

The Rafidite army admitted in a statement the killing of the officer and his aide, and the enemy’s statement said the officer held the position of “commander of the communications command of the 11th Infantry Division,” as the statement described it.

The patrol was trying to reach a position of the mujahideen that had been subjected to a previous airstrike in the notorious valley, which has witnessed many joint American and Rafidite failures over the past years.

The Rafidite forces are usually keen to reach these bombardment sites in search of any image of achievement under the wings of American aircraft.

It is noted that the mujahideen in Iraq Province have launched several attacks during recent weeks in various areas, including Kirkuk, which inflicted a number of dead and wounded in the ranks of the Rafidite forces, among them officers.

Iraq’s Defense Ministry announced the deaths on October 14. Spokesman Maj. Gen. Yahya Rasool Abdullah said the ministry mourned “Brigadier General Ahat Yasser Khudair Hussein, Commander of the 11th Infantry Division’s Communications Directorate, and Warrant Officer Mutaz Shahid Abbas,” the state-run Iraqi News Agency reported. The ministry said the two were killed “by an explosive device—a remnant of the terrorist ISIS gangs—while inspecting the site of an airstrike that had targeted a terrorist cell safehouse in Wadi Zaghytoun, Kirkuk Governorate.”

The name ISIS printed, “Ahat Yasir Husayn,” matches the ministry’s, minus the middle name Khudair. The rank, the unit and the date match too. The difference is the device. ISIS says its fighters detonated a bomb on a patrol. The ministry called it a remnant left behind by ISIS. A Kurdish broadcaster, Channel 8, noted that the available information “does not establish who planted the device or confirm responsibility for the blast,” in its report.

The day before, Iraq’s Security Media Cell said F-16s had carried out three strikes on ISIS hideouts in the valley, the Iraqi News Agency reported. A security source told 964media the unit had entered the valley “to inspect the site and recover the bodies of two militants killed in Tuesday’s F-16 strike when the device went off.” Both accounts are consistent with al-Naba’s line that the patrol was heading to a bombed position.

Casualty counts vary. ISIS claims two dead and two wounded. Rudaw reported two more soldiers were injured. Channel 8 reported three wounded, and placed the blast at about 9:10 a.m., which fits al-Naba’s “Wednesday morning.” By that evening, a security source told Shafaq the toll had risen to three after Safaa Shayaa, “a member of the Iraqi Army’s 11th Division,” died of his wounds. Al-Naba, published eight days later, still counts two dead.

The valley sits in Kirkuk’s rural south, where Iraqi forces have repeatedly targeted ISIS hideouts. On August 11, three soldiers, including two officers, were wounded by an explosive device while inspecting bombed sites southwest of Daquq, and in early July a Counter-Terrorism Service officer was killed in a clash with ISIS militants north of Kirkuk, 964media reported. Local outlet Plus4 placed the October 14 blast near the village of al-Awashra in al-Rashad subdistrict.

Nigeria: The Kanamma Ambush and a Wrecked Truck

The West Africa claims fill most of the issue. The centerpiece is an ambush on Monday, October 19, on the road from Kanamma to Geidam in Yobe State. On October 21, Amaq News Agency, the group’s news agency, published the claim. It reads:

Seven Nigerian soldiers, including a senior officer, were killed in a complex ambush in Yobe State in northern Nigeria. Military sources told Amaq News Agency that Islamic State fighters ambushed a Nigerian army patrol on the road between Kanema and Gidam two days ago after conducting reconnaissance.

The sources explained that the fighters detonated an improvised explosive device (IED) targeting the patrol, then opened heavy fire and pursued the soldiers into the surrounding area. The sources confirmed that the ambush resulted in the death of the commander of the Kanema camp and at least six of his soldiers, with others wounded and one of their vehicles disabled.

The sources reported that the fighters seized a second vehicle, two heavy machine guns, a rocket launcher, and 13 rifles before returning safely to their bases.

Al-Naba issue 570 runs its own version of the claim, calling the dead commander “a high-ranking officer”:

مقتل قائد معسكر (كانما) و٦ عناصر بكمين آخر

وفي سياق متصل، شهدت منطقة (يوبي) كمينا نوعيا موفقا لدورية للجيش النيجيري المرتد، وسقط فيه عدد من القتلى بينهم قيادي بارز.

حيث نصب جنود الخلافة في نهار يوم الاثنين (٨/جمادى الأولى) كمينا محكما لدورية للجيش النيجيري المرتد، كانت في طريقها من معسكر بلدة (كانما) تجاه بلدة (غيدام) في منطقة (يوبي).

وكان المجاهدون قد زرعوا عبوة ناسفة في طريق سير الدورية بين البلدتين، وفور وصولها لموقع الكمين، فجّروا عليها العبوة الناسفة وباشروها بنيران أسلحتهم الرشاشة.

وأسفر الكمين عن مقتل قائد المعسكر وهو ضابط برتبة عالية وستة من عناصره وإصابة آخرين وفرارهم من المواجهة، وإعطاب آلية رباعية الدفع.

واغتنم المجاهدون آلية رباعية الدفع ثانية إلى جانب رشاشين ثقيلين وقاذف صاروخي و١٣ بندقية وذخائر متنوعة، وعادوا إلى مواقعهم سالمين، ولله الحمد.

Killing of the commander of the (Kanama) camp and 6 members in another ambush

In a related context, the (Yobe) area witnessed a successful special ambush on a patrol of the apostate Nigerian army, in which a number were killed, among them a prominent leader.

The soldiers of the caliphate laid a tight ambush in the daytime on Monday (8 Jumada al-Ula) for a patrol of the apostate Nigerian army that was on its way from the camp of the town of (Kanama) toward the town of (Geidam) in the (Yobe) area.

The mujahideen had planted an explosive device in the patrol’s path between the two towns, and as soon as it reached the ambush site, they detonated the explosive device on it and engaged it with fire from their machine guns.

The ambush resulted in the killing of the camp commander, who is a high-ranking officer, and six of his members, the wounding of others and their flight from the confrontation, and the disabling of a four-wheel-drive vehicle.

The mujahideen seized a second four-wheel-drive vehicle along with two heavy machine guns, a rocket launcher, 13 rifles and assorted ammunition, and returned to their positions safely, praise be to God.

Nigerian outlets reported the ambush the same day. “The commanding officer and three other soldiers in the first vehicle died in the initial attack, while three additional soldiers were killed with scores of the attackers during the ensuing exchange of gunfire,” a source told Vanguard. Yerwa Express News said the convoy was led by a major who was the commanding officer, and corrected its time of attack to 8 a.m. AFP’s military source said “at least five” soldiers were killed when their vehicle hit a landmine in Bulabuwarna village, about seven kilometers from Geidam, where troops were then ambushed. The Cabal reported eight soldiers wounded, and said neither the security agencies nor the Yobe State government had issued a statement.

The press accounts and the ISIS claim line up on the commander’s death, the roadside bomb, the follow-up gunfire and a toll of roughly six or seven. Nigerian accounts also say troops inflicted heavy losses on the attackers, which ISIS does not mention. The Nigerian Army has not publicly named the officer or released a toll, The Cabal reported.

The truck’s marking points to a unit. The Nigerian Army has said troops of 159 Battalion were deployed at a forward operating base in Kanamma, in a 2019 statement on a repelled attack there. There is no public confirmation that the battalion is still based there or that the commander killed on October 19 led it.

Amaq’s claim ends by noting “a senior officer and 15 soldiers were killed a few days ago.” Al-Naba’s two-month West Africa infographic claims “15 killed, among them an officer,” in an attack on an army camp in the town of Kukawa, Borno, on 25 Rabi al-Akhir, or October 6. The Nigerian Army said soldiers were killed in a “coordinated multi-directional assault” in Kukawa on October 8, TheCable reported.

West Africa: Mairari, Gamboru and Adamawa

Al-Naba’s front page claims more than 32 Nigerian soldiers killed this week, “among them two prominent officers,” plus five allied militiamen and eight soldiers from neighboring Niger. It claims a camp and a position burned and 23 vehicles destroyed, disabled or seized. The front page counts 22 destroyed plus one seized; the inside page counts 23 destroyed plus one seized. The front-page lead reads:

عشرات القتلى من قوات نيجيريا والنيجر وإحراق معسكر و٢٣ آلية بهجمات متواصلة في غرب إفريقية

مُنيت القوات النيجيرية والنيجرية بخسائر فادحة خلال هذا الأسبوع، إثر كمائن وهجمات عنيفة شنها جنود الخلافة على مواقعها العسكرية ودورياتها المتنقلة، خلّفت أكثر من ٣٢ قتيلا من الجيش النيجيري المرتد بينهم ضابطان بارزان بخلاف الجرحى، إضافة لخمسة قتلى من الميليشيات الموالية لهم، بينما قُتل ثمانية عناصر من جيش النيجر المرتد، وأسفرت عن إحراق معسكر وموقع وتدمير وإعطاب ٢٢ آلية متنوعة بينها مدرعات، واغتنام آلية أخرى، في حين أُحرقت ثلاث كنائس وقريتان للنصارى المحاربين، وتوزعت الهجمات على مناطق: (برنو) و(يوبي) و(أداماوا) في شمال شرق نيجيريا وامتدت إلى منطقة (ديفا) في جنوب شرق النيجر.

Dozens killed among the forces of Nigeria and Niger, and a camp and 23 vehicles burned in continuing attacks in West Africa

The Nigerian and Nigerien forces suffered heavy losses during this week, following ambushes and violent attacks launched by the soldiers of the caliphate on their military positions and mobile patrols, which left more than 32 dead from the apostate Nigerian army, among them two prominent officers, aside from the wounded, in addition to five dead from the militias loyal to them, while eight members of the apostate Niger army were killed. They resulted in the burning of a camp and a position, the destruction and disabling of 22 assorted vehicles, among them armored vehicles, and the seizure of another vehicle, while three churches and two villages of the warring Christians were burned. The attacks were spread across the areas of (Borno), (Yobe) and (Adamawa) in northeast Nigeria and extended to the (Diffa) area in southeast Niger.

The largest single claim is a second raid in under a month on the army camp at Mairari, in Borno’s Guzamala area, on the evening of Tuesday, October 13. Al-Naba claims more than an hour of fighting, an officer serving as “deputy camp commander” killed along with 20 others, including five militiamen, and the camp burned with five armored vehicles, two trucks and 11 pickups inside.

مجددا.. اقتحام معسكر (ميراري)

وفي التفاصيل، بتوفيق الله تعالى، شهدت بلدة (ميراري) بمنطقة (برنو) هجوما داميا على أحد معسكرات الجيش النيجيري وميليشياته، خلّف أكثر من ٢٠ قتيلا بينهم ضابط كبير إلى جانب الخسائر المادية الكبيرة.

حيث شنّ جنود الخلافة هجوما مباغتا في مساء يوم الثلاثاء (٢/جمادى الأولى) على معسكر مشترك للجيش النيجيري المرتد والميليشيات الموالية له.

وهذا الهجوم هو الثاني الذي يستهدف نفس المعسكر خلال أقل من شهر، ويتعرض فيه العدو لخسائر متلاحقة ومضاعفة.

ودارت بينهم اشتباكات عنيفة بمختلف أنواع الأسلحة لمدة تزيد عن ساعة، انتهت باقتحام المجاهدين المعسكر والسيطرة عليه.

وأسفر الهجوم عن مقتل ضابط يشغل منصب "نائب قائد المعسكر" إضافة إلى ٢٠ عنصرا بينهم خمسة من الميليشيات المحلية الكافرة وإصابة آخرين وفرارهم من المواجهة.

وأحرق المجاهدون ثكنات المعسكر بالكامل إلى جانب خمس مدرّعات وشاحنتين و١١ آلية رباعية الدفع داخله.

Again.. Storming of the (Mairari) camp

In detail, by the grace of God Almighty, the town of (Mairari) in the (Borno) area witnessed a bloody attack on one of the camps of the Nigerian army and its militias, which left more than 20 dead, among them a senior officer, along with heavy material losses.

The soldiers of the caliphate launched a surprise attack on the evening of Tuesday (2 Jumada al-Ula) on a joint camp of the apostate Nigerian army and the militias loyal to it.

This attack is the second to target the same camp in less than a month, in which the enemy is suffering successive and multiplying losses.

Fierce clashes with all kinds of weapons raged between them for more than an hour, ending with the mujahideen storming the camp and taking control of it.

The attack resulted in the killing of an officer holding the position of “deputy camp commander,” in addition to 20 members, among them five from the local infidel militias, and the wounding of others and their flight from the confrontation.

The mujahideen burned the camp’s barracks entirely, along with five armored vehicles, two trucks and 11 four-wheel-drive vehicles inside it.

That raid is the best documented. A military officer told AFP that 15 soldiers and five members of the Civilian Joint Task Force were killed, NTA reported. Military sources told Reuters at least 10 soldiers were killed and 20 wounded, and on October 17 the group claimed 21 killed on Telegram, according to a Reuters report carried by Devdiscourse. Al-Naba’s 20-plus-one figure matches the claim Reuters reported.

Al-Naba also claims a Saturday, October 17, roadside bomb on a patrol between Logomani and Gamboru, in Borno, that destroyed a pickup “and killed those inside.”

قتلى آخرون وتدمير آلية بكمين متفجّر في (برنو)

على صعيد الكمائن المتفجّرة، كمن جنود الخلافة في نهار يوم السبت (٦/جمادى الأولى) لدورية للجيش النيجيري المرتد، على الطريق الرابط بين بلدتي (لوغماني) و(غمبورو) بمنطقة (برنو).

وفور وصول العدو للمكان باشرهم المجاهدون بنيران أسلحتهم الرشاشة وفجّروا عليهم عبوة ناسفة، ما أسفر عن تدمير آلية رباعية الدفع ومقتل من فيها.

Others killed and a vehicle destroyed in an explosive ambush in (Borno)

On the front of explosive ambushes, the soldiers of the caliphate lay in wait in the daytime on Saturday (6 Jumada al-Ula) for a patrol of the apostate Nigerian army, on the road linking the towns of (Logomani) and (Gamboru) in the (Borno) area.

As soon as the enemy reached the spot, the mujahideen engaged them with fire from their machine guns and detonated an explosive device on them, resulting in the destruction of a four-wheel-drive vehicle and the killing of those inside it.

AFP’s sources said eight soldiers died that day when their vehicle hit a mine near Kinuba, about 25 kilometers from Gamboru. “All eight soldiers in the vehicle were killed in the explosion,” a military officer said, according to Vanguard.

In Adamawa, the issue claims an attack on an army position at “Tilabala” on October 17 that killed three soldiers, and raids on two villages it calls Christian, with three churches burned. One of those raids, on a village al-Naba spells “Zaghulakulwa” on Thursday, October 15, appears to match a reported attack that night on Dzangola Kilba, in Adamawa’s Gombi area.

إحراق قرية نصرانية وكنيسة في (أداماوا)

وفي سياق استهداف النصارى المحاربين، داهم جنود الخلافة في يوم الخميس (٤/جمادى الأولى) قرية (زاغولاكلووا) النصرانية في منطقة (أداماوا)، ما أدى لفرارهم، وإحراق منازل القرية بالكامل إضافة إلى كنيسة، وعاد المجاهدون إلى مواقعهم سالمين، ولله الحمد.

Burning of a Christian village and a church in (Adamawa)

In the context of targeting the warring Christians, the soldiers of the caliphate on Thursday (4 Jumada al-Ula) raided the Christian village of (Zaghulakulwa) in the (Adamawa) area, causing them to flee, and burned the village’s homes entirely in addition to a church, and the mujahideen returned to their positions safely, praise be to God.

Gunmen burned eight houses, seven shops and part of a church in Dzangola Kilba and killed an elderly man, EYN News reported. The army-position claim could not be independently confirmed.

Niger: Eight Soldiers Claimed, None Confirmed

The issue claims eight Nigerien soldiers killed in the Diffa region on the border with Nigeria. Seven, it says, died when a bomb hit a pickup returning from a Nigerian army camp, on Sunday, 30 Rabi al-Akhir, or October 11. It says an eighth was shot dead in his home in Diffa city on “Monday (7 Jumada al-Ula).” That date fell on a Sunday, October 18, by the Egyptian astronomy institute’s calendar.

٧ قتلى من قوات النيجر وتدمير آلية في (ديفا)

ومن نيجيريا إلى النيجر، حيث شهد يوم الأحد (٣٠/ربيع الآخر) تفجيرا قويا على إحدى الدوريات التابعة لقوات النيجر المرتدة على الطريق الحدودي بين البلدين، مخلّفا عددا من القتلى إلى جانب آلية مدمرة.

حيث فجّر جنود الخلافة عبوة ناسفة على دورية لجيش النيجر المرتد، كانت في طريق عودتها من أحد معسكرات الجيش النيجيري المرتد القريبة من الحدود، قبل أن يضربها التفجير في منطقة (ديفا)، وأسفر التفجير النوعي عن تدمير آلية رباعية الدفع ومقتل سبعة عناصر كانوا على متنها، ولله الحمد والمنة.

قتيل آخر من جيش النيجر

على صعيد آخر، داهم جنود الخلافة في يوم الاثنين (٧/جمادى الأولى) منزل أحد عناصر جيش النيجر المرتد، داخل مدينة (ديفا)، وأطلقوا النار عليه فأردوه قتيلا على الفور قبل أن يعودوا إلى مواقعهم سالمين، ولله الحمد.

7 killed from the forces of Niger and a vehicle destroyed in (Diffa)

From Nigeria to Niger, where Sunday (30 Rabi al-Akhir) saw a powerful bombing on one of the patrols of the apostate Niger forces on the border road between the two countries, leaving a number dead along with a destroyed vehicle.

The soldiers of the caliphate detonated an explosive device on a patrol of the apostate Niger army that was on its way back from one of the apostate Nigerian army’s camps near the border, before the bombing struck it in the (Diffa) area. The special bombing resulted in the destruction of a four-wheel-drive vehicle and the killing of seven members who were aboard it, praise and thanks be to God.

Another killed from the Niger army

On another front, the soldiers of the caliphate on Monday (7 Jumada al-Ula) raided the home of a member of the apostate Niger army inside the city of (Diffa) and opened fire on him, killing him instantly, before returning to their positions safely, praise be to God.

No Nigerien or Nigerian report of either incident has surfaced. Niger’s operations center said the first half of October was “globalement calme et sous contrôle,” in a bulletin carried by the national news agency ANP. That bulletin reported troops killed four Boko Haram fighters near N’Guigmi airfield on October 9 and listed no army losses.

Pakistan and Congo

ISIS’s Khorasan Province claims five attacks in Pakistan’s Bajaur district, near the Afghan border, killing five people and wounding two. It lists a policeman, three soldiers, two men it calls spies for Pakistani intelligence and a member of the Lashkar-e-Taiba militant group. The first is a shooting in the village of Asil Targao, in Barang, on Monday, October 12:

مقتل عنصر في الشرطة في (بارانغ)

وفي التفاصيل، بتوفيق الله تعالى استهدف جنود الخلافة في يوم الاثنين (١/جمادى الأولى) عنصرا من الشرطة الباكستانية المرتدة، في قرية (أصيل ترغاو) بمنطقة (بارانغ) في (باجور)، بطلقات مسدس، ما أدى لمقتله، وعاد المجاهدون إلى مواقعهم سالمين، ولله الحمد.

Killing of a police member in (Barang)

In detail, by the grace of God Almighty, the soldiers of the caliphate on Monday (1 Jumada al-Ula) targeted a member of the apostate Pakistani police in the village of (Asil Targao) in the (Barang) area of (Bajaur) with pistol shots, killing him, and the mujahideen returned to their positions safely, praise be to God.

A police official told Dawn that assailants killed Constable Irshad Khan in “the hilly Asil Targao area” that day, and that no one had claimed responsibility. The Terrorism Research & Analysis Consortium logged the last of the five, the October 19 killing of an intelligence officer in Kohei village, Salarzai, as an ISIS-Khorasan assassination.

In Congo, ISIS’s Central Africa Province claims one attack, on Monday, October 19: a raid on an army barracks in the village of “Mongwiko,” in Ituri, which it says the soldiers fled before fighters burned it. No casualties are claimed.

إحراق ثكنة للقوات الكونغولية الصليبية بهجوم لجنود الخلافة في شمال شرق الكونغو

أحرق جنود الخلافة في ولاية وسط إفريقية خلال هذا الأسبوع ثكنة للجيش الكونغولي الصليبي بهجوم جديد في إحدى قرى منطقة (إيتوري) في شمال شرق الكونغو.

وفي التفاصيل، بتوفيق الله تعالى شنّ جنود الخلافة هجوما في يوم الاثنين (٨/جمادى الأولى) على ثكنة للجيش الكونغولي الصليبي، في قرية (مونغويكو) بمنطقة (إيتوري)، بالأسلحة الرشاشة، ما أدى لفرارهم، وإحراق الثكنة، واغتنام قاذف صاروخي وذخائر، ولله الحمد.

Burning of a barracks of the crusader Congolese forces in an attack by the soldiers of the caliphate in northeast Congo

The soldiers of the caliphate in Central Africa Province this week burned a barracks of the crusader Congolese army in a new attack on one of the villages of the (Ituri) area in northeast Congo.

In detail, by the grace of God Almighty, the soldiers of the caliphate on Monday (8 Jumada al-Ula) launched an attack on a barracks of the crusader Congolese army in the village of (Mongwiko) in the (Ituri) area with machine guns, causing them to flee, and burned the barracks and seized a rocket launcher and ammunition, praise be to God.

The issue says nothing about the killing of 10 people at mining sites in Ituri’s Mambasa territory overnight into October 16, in an attack attributed to the Allied Democratic Forces, which fight under ISIS’s Central Africa banner, Actualite.cd reported.

The Weekly Tally

Page two of the issue is al-Naba’s regular “Harvest of the Soldiers” infographic. It covers attacks “published during a week,” from 4 to 10 Jumada al-Ula, or October 15 to 21. Because it counts claims by publication date, it likely includes the October 19 Kanamma ambush and may include earlier attacks published that week, such as Mairari.

The full page breaks the 57 down as 52 people it calls “infidels and apostates,” four it labels with its anti-Shiite slur and one it calls a “crusader.” By province, it lists 11 operations and 46 killed and wounded in West Africa, five operations and seven casualties in Khorasan, one operation and four casualties in Iraq, all in Kirkuk, and one operation in Central Africa. It lists no attack in Syria, now governed by a former jihadist, or in Somalia, Mozambique, the Sahel or Yemen.

A separate back-page infographic tallies two months of West Africa claims, from 10 Rabi al-Awwal to 10 Jumada al-Ula, or August 23 to October 21. It claims 76 attacks and 231 killed and wounded, “among them five commanders.” The United Nations Monitoring Team called ISWAP the most active ISIS affiliate, saying member states reported it conducted “over 174 attacks” from January to May, in its August 2026 report.

The Front Page

The masthead reads “Issue 570 | Year Eighteen | Thursday 11 Jumada al-Ula 1448 AH | 8 pages.” The Islamic dates check out. Egypt’s National Research Institute of Astronomy and Geophysics put the first of Jumada al-Ula 1448 on Monday, October 12, al-Masry al-Youm reported. That makes 3 Jumada al-Ula Wednesday, October 14, the day of the Kirkuk blast, and 11 Jumada al-Ula Thursday, October 22. Issue 569 carried 4 Jumada al-Ula, one week earlier.

The “eighteenth year” counts from the paper’s origins. Al-Naba “has existed in various forms since 2010, when it was first introduced by ISI, the Islamic State’s predecessor,” the International Centre for the Study of Radicalisation found in 2018. That year was 1431 on the Islamic calendar, which makes 1448 the eighteenth. The same report counted 135 weekly editions by June 2018. The 435 issues since then match, almost exactly, the number of weeks that have passed.

The Editorial: “Their Enmity Increases Our Certainty”

The issue’s editorial, on page three, is titled “Their Enmity Increases Our Certainty.” It argues that each campaign against the group has hardened it, and casts ISIS fighters as heirs of the prophets in a long war with “the nations of unbelief.”

“Whenever they thought they had extinguished the fire of jihad with their campaigns and raids, the mujahideen emerged more steadfast and tougher, rising from among the rubble,” the editorial says. “The attempts of God’s enemies to eliminate the Islamic State, to abolish its project, or to shake its emirs and bend their resolve have failed,” it adds. It tells the believer to know “that he is an army, even if he is alone,” and says he is commanded “to terrorize the enemies of God by every means.”

A page-seven essay on God as provider makes a related point about money. It mocks world powers for boasting of their weapons and budgets while suffering “financial problems, economic collapses, and turmoil in stocks and currencies,” and says God provides for the fighters in spite of the unbelievers’ siege, “even by the hands of the unbelievers themselves, willingly or unwillingly, through money and through fighting.”

That message reaches an Iraq that the United States has largely left. On September 30, U.S. Central Command completed its departure from Erbil Air Base, ending the Operation Inherent Resolve mission in Iraq, and its commander, Adm. Brad Cooper, said ISIS “no longer poses a systemic threat to Iraq’s national security,” according to the command’s release. The UN Monitoring Team assessed in August that ISIS in Iraq was “at its weakest” and numbered in “the low hundreds of fighters able to carry out unsophisticated operations.”

The newspaper has outlasted years of platform takedowns. The same UN report said ISIS propaganda “remained centred around its weekly newsletter, Al-Naba.” For readers in Kirkuk, issue 570 is a reminder of a different durability. Two weeks after U.S. forces left, the Defense Ministry mourned a brigadier general of the 11th Division, and Shafaq News reported that a third soldier, Safaa Shayaa, had died of his wounds.
